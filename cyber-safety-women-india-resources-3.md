# Cyber Safety & Awareness Resources for Women in India

Compiled from the Lok Sabha Committee on Empowerment of Women's Fourth Report on "Cyber Crimes and Cyber Safety of Women" (March 2026) and public sources. Organised by theme.

## 1. Reporting & Emergency Helplines
*Where to go the moment something happens.*

**National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (NCRP)** (India — Govt. of India) — [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in)
24x7 portal to report any cybercrime; has an anonymous option specifically for "Women/Child Related Crime."

**Cybercrime Helpline — 1930** (India — Govt. of India)
Toll-free number for financial cyber fraud; agents can alert banks in real time to freeze fraudulent transactions.

**NCW Women Helpline — 14490** (India — Govt. of India) — [ncwwomenhelpline.ncw.gov.in](https://ncwwomenhelpline.ncw.gov.in)
24x7 helpline for women facing violence (including online); links callers to police, hospitals, One Stop Centres, counsellors.

**Report & Check Suspect** (India — Govt. of India; feature on cybercrime.gov.in)
Lets citizens search known cybercriminal identifiers (numbers, emails, URLs) and report suspicious ones.

**Women Helpline — 181 ("Sakhi" One Stop Centres)** (India — Ministry of Women & Child Development)
Integrated support scheme — police facilitation, medical aid, legal aid, counselling and shelter under one roof for women facing violence; cyber harassment/stalking cases can be routed here alongside the cyber-specific 1930/NCRP.

**NALSA Free Legal Aid Helpline — 15100** (India — Govt. of India) — [nalsa.gov.in](https://nalsa.gov.in)
National Legal Services Authority; connects women (especially those who can't afford a lawyer) to a free panel advocate, including for cybercrime cases.

## 2. Legal — Rights, Laws & Grievance Redressal
*What the law says, and how to escalate if a platform doesn't act.*

**Grievance Appellate Committee (GAC)** (India — Govt. of India) — [gac.gov.in](https://www.gac.gov.in)
Government appeal mechanism under IT Rules, 2021, for when a platform's own Grievance Officer fails to remove unlawful content (NCII, deepfakes, obscenity, impersonation) or doesn't respond in time.

**NCW Her Legal Guide** (India — NCW; app & web, English/Hindi)
Explains women's legal rights and relevant statutes/helplines in plain language — a quick legal reference in a crisis.

**IT Rules, 2021 (Intermediary Guidelines)** (India — Govt. of India) — [text of the rules](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_CEN_45_76_00001_200021_1517807324077&type=rule&filename=information_technology_(intermediary_guidelines_and_digital_media_ethics_code)_rules,_2021_(updated_06.04.2023)-.pdf)
Legal framework requiring platforms to take down NCII/deepfake content within 2 hours of a verified complaint, resolve serious complaints within 36 hours, and appoint India-based grievance officers.

**Key statutes** (India — background, not a "portal" but useful to link/explain on the page): IT Act, 2000 (Sections 66E, 67, 67A, 67B, 69A, 79); Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023; POCSO Act, 2012; Indecent Representation of Women (Prohibition) Act, 1986.

## 3. Cyber Awareness (General Public Campaigns)
*Mass-reach campaigns explaining scams, threats and how to stay alert.*

**CyberDost** (India — I4C/MHA social media handle — X, Instagram, YouTube, etc.)
Real-time alerts on cybercrime modus operandi (digital arrest scams, sextortion, investment fraud) and promotion of 1930/NCRP.

**Cyber Jaagrookta Diwas** (India — MHA/States-UTs)
Monthly (first Wednesday) nationwide awareness day run through States/UTs, schools, colleges and Panchayati Raj institutions.

**Digital Shakti Campaign** (India — NCW + CyberPeace Foundation, with Meta as partner) — [cyberpeace.org/initiatives/digital-shakti](https://cyberpeace.org/initiatives/digital-shakti)
Ongoing pan-India digital-skilling and awareness programme for women and girls, running in phases since 2018; includes a physical Digital Shakti Centre in New Delhi.

**Cyber Rakshak / Cyber Shiksha** (India — I4C-linked initiatives)
Named in the government report alongside Digital Shakti as programmes building cyber-safety skills among women and girls.

**e-SafeHER / "Cyber Sakhi" Programme** (India — MeitY/ISEA, with C-DAC Hyderabad & Reliance Foundation — launched 2026)
Newer initiative (postdates the March 2026 Lok Sabha report) aiming to train 1 million rural women as community-level "Cyber Sakhis" through Self-Help Groups by 2029, building last-mile cybersecurity awareness and confidence in digital transactions.

**State Police Cyber Safety Campaigns** (India — e.g. Gujarat Police's "Operation Surakshit Cyberspace")
State-run drives combining school programmes, She-Teams/Cyber Cells and community outreach on threats like deepfakes and sextortion; worth checking for an equivalent campaign in your target state.

## 4. Cyber Hygiene (Practical How-To Guidance)
*Day-to-day safe practices — passwords, privacy settings, safe browsing.*

**ISEA — Information Security Education & Awareness** (India — MeitY) — [isea.gov.in](https://www.isea.gov.in) / [staysafeonline.in](https://staysafeonline.in)
MeitY's core cyber-hygiene project; publishes downloadable handbooks and multilingual posters/videos on passwords, privacy settings, safe social media use, and scam recognition — several written specifically for women (e.g. "Information Security Awareness Handbook for Women," "Cyber Security Tips for Women," "Online Safety Tips for Women at Home").

**CERT-In "Cyber Security Handbook for Mahila Suraksha"** (India — Govt. of India) — [cert-in.org.in](https://www.cert-in.org.in)
Practical handbook released on International Women's Day 2025 with hygiene tips aimed at women.

**Emerging Cyber Threats Against Women and Girls** (India — ISEA) — [staysafeonline.in/concept/emerging-cyber-threats-against-women-and-girls](https://www.staysafeonline.in/concept/emerging-cyber-threats-against-women-and-girls)
A specific resource page cataloguing newer threat types (deepfakes, sextortion, doxing) with prevention tips.

## 5. Platform Safety Tools (Non-Government)
*Tools run by platforms/global NGOs that individuals can use directly.*

**StopNCII.org** (Global — StopNCII is run by the UK-based SWGfL; works for adults worldwide, including India-usable)
Global tool to hash your own intimate images so participating platforms can proactively block them from being shared without your consent — referenced by the Lok Sabha Committee as a possible model for an Indian equivalent.

**Take It Down** (USA — NCMEC, with Meta funding; India-usable) — [takeitdown.ncmec.org](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org)
Sister tool to StopNCII, but specifically for images/videos of **under-18s**. Lets a minor (or a parent, anonymously) hash an intimate image so participating platforms (Meta, Pornhub, OnlyFans, Yubo) can detect and block it — the image itself is never uploaded or viewed. Directly relevant to deepfake/sextortion cases involving minors, which the Lok Sabha report flags as a growing concern.

**Meta Safety Centre** (USA — Meta; India-usable, applies to Facebook/Instagram) — [about.meta.com/actions/safety](https://about.meta.com/actions/safety)
Reporting tools for NCII, sextortion and harassment on Facebook/Instagram; monthly India transparency reports at [transparency.meta.com](https://transparency.meta.com/reports/regulatory-transparency-reports/).

**Google/YouTube Safety Centre** (USA — Google; India-usable, applies to Google platforms)
Reporting and safety tools for harassment, impersonation and non-consensual content on Google platforms.

**Safecity (Red Dot Foundation)** (India) — [safecity.in](https://safecity.in)
Crowdsourced, anonymous map of harassment/abuse incidents (originally public-space focused, now also used for online harassment reporting); data is used to identify patterns and support community/police advocacy.

**Breakthrough India** (India)
NGO running youth-focused awareness campaigns on gender violence, including digital harassment, through school and college outreach.

## 6. Institutional / Enforcement Background
*Not citizen-facing tools, but useful context if your page explains "what happens after you report."*

**SAHYOG Portal** (India — I4C, MHA) — content takedown notices sent by law enforcement to intermediaries under Section 79(3)(b) of the IT Act.

**National Cyber Forensic Laboratory (NCFL), Dwarka** (India — MHA/I4C) — central forensic lab supporting investigation of cybercrimes against women and children.

**CyTrain Portal** (India — I4C) — certification platform training police/judicial officers in cybercrime investigation.

**CyberPeace Foundation** (India) — [cyberpeace.org](https://www.cyberpeace.org) — NGO partner across several government awareness and training initiatives (Digital Shakti, LEA training).

## 7. International Resources (Reference / Model Only — Not Directly Usable from India)
*These organisations' own helplines and reporting schemes are restricted to their home country, but their public guides, toolkits, and regulatory models are valuable reference material when designing India-facing content.*

**NNEDV Safety Net Project — TechSafety.org** (USA) — [techsafety.org](https://www.techsafety.org)
One of the most thorough tech-abuse toolkits globally — covers stalkerware, tech-facilitated intimate partner abuse, and survivor safety planning. The guides translate well to the Indian context for content/training design, even though the org's own services aren't accessible from India.

**eSafety Commissioner** (Australia) — [esafety.gov.au](https://www.esafety.gov.au)
Runs one of the world's most developed regulatory models for image-based abuse and adult cyber abuse. Its public education guides on image-based abuse and sextortion are a strong reference for content design, but its formal reporting/removal scheme cannot be used by Indian residents.

**Cyber Civil Rights Initiative (CCRI)** (USA) — [cybercivilrights.org](https://cybercivilrights.org)
Leading US non-profit on image-based sexual abuse. Its Safety Center guide (evidence preservation, removal steps, emotional support) is useful educational reading, but the CCRI Image Abuse Helpline itself is restricted to US-based adult victims.
